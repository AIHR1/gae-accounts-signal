# SOP --- Change Detection V2

## Purpose

Identify new external developments at accounts in scope that could
plausibly change what HR needs to deliver.

This stage deliberately casts a wider net than the final digest. It
should detect meaningful business and HR developments without
prematurely requiring an explicit AIHR angle.

It does not decide whether an item reaches the Account Manager.

## Core detection model

Look for three forms of change.

### 1. Material events

Discrete developments such as:

-   Acquisition, merger, divestment, or integration
-   Expansion into new markets, offices, geographies, or business lines
-   Major restructuring or organizational redesign
-   Significant workforce change
-   Site openings, closures, consolidations, or relocations with
    meaningful people impact
-   New executive leadership when connected to a material mandate
-   Other consequential organizational events

### 2. Strategic shifts

Changes in how the organization intends to operate or compete,
including:

-   New or materially changed corporate strategy
-   AI, automation, digital, or productivity transformation
-   Operating-model change
-   Cost, simplification, or efficiency programs
-   Workforce or talent strategy changes
-   HR or people strategy changes
-   Skills-based organization initiatives
-   HR transformation
-   Major learning, capability, leadership, or reskilling strategies

A strategic shift can qualify even without a single discrete "event."

### 3. New evidence of changing expectations

Look for credible new evidence that the organization expects people, HR,
or specific HR functions to operate differently.

Examples:

-   Leadership states that roles or ways of working are changing
-   HR is expected to become more data-driven, strategic, AI-enabled,
    consultative, or skills-focused
-   New capabilities are identified as necessary for strategy execution
-   HRBPs, Talent, L&D, People Analytics, TA, OD, or other HR functions
    receive changed responsibilities
-   A company explicitly links transformation to upskilling, reskilling,
    workforce readiness, or new competencies

This category prevents the system from requiring a neat corporate event
when the important intelligence is a newly articulated expectation.

## Research scope

Research every account row independently using the exact company name at
least once.

Use relevant legal-name, group, subsidiary, business-unit, and
local-language variants when useful, while preserving the identity of
the account row.

Search external, publicly available information.

## Discovery routes

For each account, cover at least these routes:

### Company/business change

Search for strategy, acquisition, expansion, restructuring, operating
model, transformation, AI/automation, productivity, workforce,
integration, and other material organizational change.

### HR/people change

Search for HR strategy, people strategy, talent, workforce, skills,
capability, reskilling/upskilling, learning, HR transformation, HR
operating model, and changed expectations of HR.

### Primary-source check

Review relevant company newsroom, investor-relations, filings/results,
strategy materials, and leadership communications.

When appropriate add:

-   Earnings or regulated filings
-   Local-language searches
-   Credible business/HR trade media
-   Legal-name/subsidiary variants
-   Broader date searches followed by date verification

Do not equate one unsuccessful search with a silent account.

## Source priority

Prioritize:

1.  Company newsroom, investor relations, filings, annual/interim
    results, and strategy materials
2.  CEO, CHRO, senior HR, Talent, L&D, transformation, or relevant
    business-leader communications
3.  Credible business and HR trade media with clear attribution
4.  Official company social posts when dated and attributable
5.  Other reliable sources that reveal a meaningful change

## Evidence that is not sufficient by itself

Do not treat the following as standalone digest-worthy changes:

-   A cluster of HR/L&D/HRIS vacancies
-   A new CHRO or senior HR appointment without a meaningful mandate
-   An HR-tech implementation or go-live without evidence of changed
    people/process/capability expectations
-   Generic leadership commentary
-   Awards, rankings, employer-brand content
-   Generic claims about people being important
-   Routine company announcements with no plausible HR consequence

These may still be recorded as evidence for enrichment.

## Candidate threshold

Pass a candidate to Evidence Enrichment when:

-   Something new or materially updated exists within the review period;
    and
-   There is a plausible, not necessarily proven, path to changed HR
    priorities, responsibilities, or capability requirements.

Do not require the HR implication to be explicit yet.

Do not pass routine activity merely because it contains HR keywords.

## Candidate handoff

For every candidate record:

-   Account
-   Date
-   Source
-   Type: Material Event / Strategic Shift / Changing Expectations
-   Concise description of what changed
-   Initial reason it could plausibly affect HR
-   What remains unknown
-   Questions Evidence Enrichment should investigate

Do not create an AIHR sales pitch.

If no candidate clears the threshold, mark the account silent in the
internal execution record.
