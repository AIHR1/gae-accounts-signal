# SOP --- Account Reasoning & Relevance V2

## Purpose

Translate verified external intelligence into an account-specific
hypothesis that is useful to an AIHR Account Manager.

This is the strict gate between research and the Account Manager's
inbox.

## Inputs

For each candidate use:

### AIHR context

AIHR helps organizations build the capabilities of HR professionals
through professional development, online learning, capability
assessment, structured learning journeys, and related learning/advisory
solutions.

### Account Manager context

The Account Manager manages and grows existing AIHR customer
relationships through retention, renewal, expansion, value realization,
and relevant customer engagement.

### Account context

Use the account row:

-   Company name
-   Rep's Name (the Account Manager who owns the account)
-   Primary use case
-   Breadth of AIHR reach within HR
-   Highest meaningful HR access

### Evidence package

Use only facts and supported hypotheses from Evidence Enrichment. Do not
invent missing customer context.

## Reasoning sequence

### Step 1 --- State the meaningful change

Identify whether the candidate represents:

-   A material event
-   A strategic shift
-   New evidence of changing expectations

State it factually.

If there is no meaningful new or materially updated development from the
active review period, suppress.

### Step 2 --- Translate it to HR

Ask:

**Because of this change, what might HR need to deliver differently?**

Potential implications can include workforce planning, organizational
design, change/adoption, talent strategy, leadership, skills strategy,
HR data/AI, learning, culture integration, HR operating model, or
another HR outcome.

Do not assume every transformation creates an AIHR opportunity.

### Step 3 --- Translate HR to HR professionals

Ask:

**If that implication is correct, what might relevant HR professionals
need to do differently?**

Be specific about responsibilities, decisions, behaviors, knowledge, or
capabilities.

Examples of useful specificity:

-   HRBPs may need to advise leaders on workforce redesign rather than
    only execute established workforce plans.
-   TA may need stronger executive-market mapping if specialist search
    capability is moving in-house.
-   People Analytics teams may need stronger data-governance capability
    if decision-making is shifting toward skills/data models.

Avoid generic conclusions such as:

-   HR must adapt
-   HR needs to become strategic
-   Employees need upskilling
-   AIHR can help with learning

### Step 4 --- Apply account context

The same external development can be relevant to one AIHR relationship
and irrelevant to another.

#### Primary use case

Ask how close the inferred capability need is to what AIHR currently
helps the customer achieve.

For a narrow **Specific skill gap**, require a relatively direct
functional/capability connection.

For **Ongoing team upskilling**, test whether the development changes
the capabilities needed by the teams AIHR reaches.

For **Broader HR capability building**, broader changes to HR
expectations may be relevant when they plausibly affect the HR community
in scope.

For **Enterprise HR capability transformation**, enterprise-level HR
operating/capability changes may have strong fit, subject to evidence.

For **Unclear**, do not assume broad relevance; require stronger
evidence.

#### Breadth of AIHR reach within HR

For a **Specific HR function**, require a direct connection to that
function.

For **Multiple HR functions**, cross-functional HR developments may fit.

For **Most/all of HR**, broad changes in HR capability expectations may
be relevant.

For a **Small group within HR**, require a close connection to the
actual group/use case.

For **Unclear**, avoid broad assumptions.

#### Highest meaningful HR access

Use this to test whether the Account Manager can credibly discuss the
implication with existing stakeholders.

Do not treat CHRO-level source seniority as relevance.

A CHRO statement at a global company can still be too distant from a
CHRO -2/-3 relationship if the implication does not plausibly cascade to
the stakeholders AIHR reaches.

Conversely, a senior-level strategy can be useful when it clearly
changes expectations for those stakeholders.

## Scope safeguard: local and business-unit evidence

Do not suppress evidence solely because it is local, site-level, country-level, or limited to one business unit.

Assess whether the evidence provides concrete proof of how a broader organizational or strategic change is affecting roles, skills, ways of working, workforce decisions, or HR responsibilities.

Preserve the supported scope. A local example may strengthen a hypothesis about a broader change, but do not generalize the local effect to the whole enterprise unless the evidence supports that conclusion.

## Required hypothesis

Before inclusion, complete:

**Because \[external change\] happened, \[specific HR/people
implication\] may become relevant. This matters to this AIHR
relationship because \[specific account-context connection\]. The
Account Manager can test this by exploring \[specific
hypothesis/question\].**

If this cannot be completed credibly and specifically, suppress.

### Failing example

"AIHR could support the company with upskilling."

This is generic and does not establish account relevance.

### Passing pattern

"The operating-model change may increase the need for HRBPs to support
workforce redesign and change. Because AIHR already works across
multiple HR functions on broader HR capability building, it is credible
to test whether these changes are creating new capability expectations
for the HR community."

## Include / Suppress decision

### Include only when all are true

-   A meaningful new external change exists.
-   The HR implication is credible.
-   The people-level implication is specific.
-   Account context creates a credible relationship fit.
-   The hypothesis is useful enough that the Account Manager does not
    need to perform substantial additional interpretation.
-   The resulting conversation would feel consultative rather than
    opportunistic.

### Suppress when any is true

-   It is merely interesting company news.
-   The only link is that AIHR sells learning/upskilling.
-   The implication is too distant from the current AIHR relationship.
-   A senior leader said something but it does not translate credibly to
    the stakeholder population.
-   Hiring is the only evidence of meaningful change.
-   A leadership appointment has no relevant mandate/change.
-   HR technology is the only development and no changed
    people/process/capability expectation is supported.
-   Several unsupported assumptions are required to manufacture
    relevance.
-   The proposed conversation angle is essentially a product pitch
    disguised as a question.

## Action label

Choose exactly one for included items.

### Proactive outreach

Use when the external change plus account fit provides a credible reason
to contact the customer.

### Use in conversation

Use when the intelligence materially improves the Account Manager's
understanding or questioning but is better used in an existing/future
conversation.

Do not use Monitor.

## Conversation angle

Prefer hypothesis testing:

1.  Briefly reference the known development.
2.  Frame the implication tentatively.
3.  Ask whether the stakeholder is seeing the same effect.
4.  Explore whether it creates a capability-development need.

Do not jump immediately to an AIHR solution.

## Output to Master Digest

For every included item return:

-   Account
-   Account Manager
-   Action label
-   What changed
-   Why this matters for this relationship
-   Hypothesis to test
-   Suggested conversation angle
-   1--3 strongest evidence sources

For suppressed items return only a concise suppression reason to the
internal execution record.
