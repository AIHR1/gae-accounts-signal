# SOP --- Evidence Enrichment V2

## Purpose

Investigate candidates from Change Detection and establish what the
available evidence supports about their HR, workforce, and capability
implications.

This stage turns a raw development into a reliable evidence package. It
does not make the final commercial relevance decision.

## Core question

For each candidate ask:

**What is actually changing, and what evidence helps explain what this
may require from HR and HR professionals?**

## Enrichment sequence

### 1. Establish the development

Confirm:

-   What happened or changed
-   Which organization/business unit/geography is affected
-   Scale
-   Timing
-   Strategic rationale where available
-   Workforce or organizational impact where available

Correct or discard the candidate if the original interpretation is
overstated.

### 2. Investigate the HR connection

Look for evidence that the development changes or may change:

-   HR priorities
-   Workforce strategy
-   Talent strategy
-   Organizational design
-   Change/adoption responsibilities
-   Skills strategy
-   Learning/reskilling
-   Leadership/management expectations
-   HR operating model
-   HR data/AI expectations
-   Responsibilities of specific HR functions

The connection may be explicit or inferred. Keep those two cases
separate.

### 3. Investigate people-level implications

Ask what relevant HR professionals may need to:

-   Do differently
-   Decide differently
-   Prioritize differently
-   Advise the business on
-   Learn or become more capable at
-   Execute at greater scale or sophistication

Avoid vague conclusions such as "HR needs to adapt."

## Evidence lenses

Use these selectively according to the candidate.

### Leadership and HR statements

Look for statements revealing:

-   New priorities
-   Changed expectations
-   Problems or constraints
-   Workforce implications
-   Capability requirements
-   Implementation expectations
-   New HR direction

Seniority alone does not create relevance. Generic thought leadership is
weak evidence.

### Skills and capability evidence

Look for:

-   Explicit skills/capability gaps
-   Reskilling/upskilling
-   Workforce readiness
-   New competencies
-   Leadership/management capability
-   HR capability requirements
-   Talent supply constraints tied to execution

Broad workforce skills evidence can be useful even when it is not
HR-specific.

### Hiring patterns

Hiring is corroborating evidence, not a standalone trigger.

Use relevant roles to test whether the company is building capabilities
such as:

-   Workforce planning
-   HR analytics/data
-   HR transformation
-   Organizational development
-   Change
-   L&D
-   Skills strategy
-   Strategic TA
-   HR technology/product ownership

Role descriptions can be more useful than vacancy counts because they
reveal changed expectations.

Do not promote a candidate merely because a numeric hiring threshold has
been reached.

### Leadership appointments

Investigate appointments for:

-   Stated mandate
-   Transformation responsibility
-   Expanded scope
-   New function
-   Strategy reset
-   Reorganization
-   Priorities relevant to the underlying change

An appointment without this evidence is context only.

### HR technology and process change

Investigate HRIS, HCM, LMS/LXP, talent, AI, analytics, or other HR
technology for evidence of:

-   Process redesign
-   Role redesign
-   New ways of working
-   New data/AI expectations
-   Adoption/change requirements
-   Changed capability expectations

A system go-live alone is insufficient.

### Business and workforce evidence

Look for:

-   Scale and geography
-   Populations/functions affected
-   Workforce changes
-   Role changes
-   Timelines
-   Executive accountability
-   Strategic rationale

This evidence helps prevent overgeneralization.

## Local and business-unit evidence

Do not discard evidence solely because it concerns one country, site, function, or business unit.

Local evidence can be valuable when it shows concretely how a broader strategy or organizational change is affecting roles, skills, ways of working, workforce decisions, or HR responsibilities.

Use it as scoped evidence. State the boundary clearly and do not infer that the same effect exists enterprise-wide without support.

## Verification standard

Prefer company-primary and regulated sources for core factual claims.

Credible secondary media may be used when:

-   Claims are clearly attributed,
-   Primary material is inaccessible or incomplete, or
-   The secondary source adds useful context that can be checked.

Open the underlying source where possible. Verify publication date and
distinguish publication date from event date.

Vendor case studies may establish customer-specific technology/process
facts when sufficiently detailed, but vendor marketing alone should not
establish a broader customer strategy.

## Triangulation

Do not require multiple sources when one strong primary source
establishes the relevant fact.

Seek corroboration when:

-   The HR implication is not explicit
-   A source is broad or ambiguous
-   A secondary source makes a consequential claim
-   Hiring, appointments, or technology are being used to infer
    direction
-   The candidate appears likely to reach the Account Manager

Triangulation exists to improve confidence, not to maximize source
count.

## Evidence package handoff

Return:

### Facts

Only directly supported claims.

### Explicit HR/capability implications

Implications stated directly by the customer or a reliable attributed
source.

### Inferred HR/capability implications

Reasoned possibilities supported by the facts. Label them as hypotheses.

### People-level implications

Specific ways relevant HR professionals may need to operate differently
if the hypothesis is correct.

### Corroborating evidence

Relevant leadership statements, skills evidence, hiring, appointments,
HR technology, workforce information, or other support.

### Scope limitations

Note geography, business-unit, population, or other boundaries that
should prevent overgeneralization.

### Confidence/gaps

What is established versus uncertain.

### Sources

Select the strongest 1--3 sources for downstream use. Retain additional
verification evidence internally.

## Stop rule

Suppress the candidate before Account Reasoning when enrichment
establishes that:

-   The original development was routine or overstated
-   Evidence is too weak to establish the underlying change
-   There is no credible path to an HR implication
-   The apparent connection depends primarily on generic HR keywords

Do not keep a candidate alive because research time has already been
invested.
